## 1. Paper Specification: What $d_\epsilon(T)$ Is and Where the Paper Uses It

1. **Per-triangle bound definition (§4.1.2, `refs/text/yang2025_hybrid_boolean.txt:354-390` R):**
   > *"For a boundary triangle T, we find the minimal rectangular region in the parametric domain that covers its three vertices. Then using the NURBS subdivision technique, we can obtain a subpatch defined on the rectangular region. We compute the maximal distance $d(T)$ between the control points of the patch and the triangle as shown in Fig. 6. Thus, we re-define the discretization error bound $d_\epsilon(T)$ of a NURBS patch defined on a parametric triangle corresponding to the mesh triangle $T$ as follows:*
   > $$d_\epsilon(T) = \begin{cases} d_\epsilon & \text{if } T \text{ is an inner triangle,} \\ d(T) & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$$
   > *An inner triangle is a triangle that does not contain sampling points on the boundaries. For convenience, $d_\epsilon(T)$ will be abbreviated as $d_\epsilon$ in the following text, with the understanding that a different value is used when dealing with boundary triangles. The distance bounds are then used in our octree search structure as explained in Sec. 4.2.1 for a conservative intersection detection."*

2. **Conservative proximity / octree AABB offset (§4.2.1, `:433-461` L and `:435-438` R; Fig. 10 `:487-495` L; §4.3.3 `:524-526` R):**
   > *"Therefore, if there are two triangles $\triangle t_A \in M_A$ and $\triangle t_B \in M_B$ such that $\mathit{Dis}(\triangle t_A, \triangle t_B) < 2d_\epsilon$, this implies potential intersections of $S_A$ and $S_B$."* (`:459-461` L)  
   > *"In our implementation, we use an octree to detect triangles that are closer than $2d_\epsilon$. For each triangle, we compute its AABB, offset the box by $d_\epsilon$, and insert it into the tree, as shown in Fig. 7. For the triangles generated from the sampling points on the boundary curves, the offset distances become $d(T)$ as calculated in Fig. 6."* (`:435-438` R)

   Combined with the §4.1.2 abbreviation rule (`:382-386` R), the paper's `$2d_\epsilon$` test on a triangle pair $(T_A, T_B)$ is $d_\epsilon(T_A) + d_\epsilon(T_B)$.

3. **Recalculating $d(T)$ in Stage-4 mesh updating (§4.4.1, `:605-609` L and `:546-572` R):**
   > *"Next, through CDT we obtain valid discretizations of the trimmed meshes, see Fig. 11. ... For the newly generated boundary triangles around the intersection curve, we recalculate $d(T)$ to maintain controllable error."* (`:557-558` R, `:568-571` R)

   Crucially, §4.4.1 **never rejects** a CDT patch or fan refill because $d(T) > d_\epsilon$ or $d(T_{\text{new}}) > d(T_{\text{old}})$ (`ChordDegradation` / `CertificateFailed { what: "chord" }`), and **never injects** an interior Steiner grid (`I2e`) to force $d(T) \le d_\epsilon$. Patch CDT is a **producer** of recalculated $d(T)$ (`stage4_dt::d_of_t`). Under the F2 owner's uniform Stage-4/5 rule, $d_\epsilon$ applies **only** to surviving unsplit Stage-1 inner triangles (whose guarantee comes from Stage 1's structured sampling, §4.1); **every** triangle created in Stage 4/5 — boundary or inner, by any mutator — carries its own recomputed $d(T)$ via `d_of_t` (`f64::NAN` if `d_of_t` fails or face attribution is absent).

### Category C — Stage-4 3D Vertex Relocation Residual & Corridor Gates (`stage4_correct.rs`, `stage4_relocate.rs`, `stage4_boundary_curve.rs`)

**Architectural context (applies to C1–C13):**  
In Yang 2025 (§4.2.3 `:510-519` L, §4.3.1–4.3.3 `:479-540`), each mesh intersection vertex is mapped by barycentric coordinates into the $(u,v)$ and $(s,t)$ domains of its two intersecting Stage-1 triangles and optimized by geometric tangent-plane step (§4.3.2) or Newton (§4.3.1) until the 3D separation between the two surface points is $< d_p = 10^{-7}$ (`:535-537` L, `:744-745` R), rejecting only if an iterate leaves the patch's parametric domain (`:529-534` R) or fails to converge (`:656-658` L). **The paper has no 3D displacement corridor (`|q - p| <= tangent_plane_corridor(d_ε, sin θ)`) or off-surface `d_ε` gate during relocation** (roadmap milestone **F3**, deviations `N3`/`N95`).

However, **while these 3D gates exist in `yang-rs` (pre-F3)**, every one of them tests an arrangement vertex `p` (produced in Stage 2 on intersecting Stage-1 triangles $T_A \in M_A, T_B \in M_B$, *before* `update_patches_ex_with_bounds` runs at `stage4_correct.rs:5709`) against how far `p` can legitimately sit off an exact surface $S$:
- Using solid-global `stage4_chord_band(a, b)` or `chord_tol_for_curved_owner` is **unsound in both directions**: (i) if an operand has both a `R = 100` barrel and an `r = 1` fillet, `stage4_chord_band` gives the `r = 1` fillet a `100×` oversized corridor (admitting wrong-branch jumps); (ii) if `p` lies on a Stage-1 boundary triangle $T$ or split arrangement sub-triangle whose $d(T) > d_\epsilon$ (e.g. an elongated cylinder/cone triangle), `p` can legitimately sit up to $d(T) > d_\epsilon$ inside $S$, so scalar `d_eps` **falsely rejects** a valid relocation (`Stage4RegionInvalid`)!
- **Why C1–C13 are marked `CONDITIONAL / UNSURE`:** If F3 replaces 3D corridor gates with parametric `(u, v)` domain clamping, C1–C13 retire altogether instead of being re-wired. If any 3D residual/corridor gate is retained (or refactored incrementally before F3), replacing solid/face scalar `d_eps` with the incident triangles' per-triangle bound $\max_{T \ni v,\, T \subset S} d_\epsilon(T)$ fixes both (i) and (ii) — **noting** that because C1–C12 run *before* `update_patches_ex_with_bounds` (`:5709`), they read the pre-CDT `TriangleErrorBoundMap` (seeded by `from_stage2_kept`), whereas C13 (`plan_boundary_relocations` at `:6260`) runs *after* `update_patches_ex_with_bounds`.
