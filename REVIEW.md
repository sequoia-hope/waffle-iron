## 1. Kernel fidelity to Yang 2025

### 1.4 Robustness

**Exact versus floating predicates.** Where it matters the arithmetic is exact: `cherchi-rs` uses Shewchuk adaptive predicates with a rational zero re-check (N24), FPG semi-static filters with interval and rational tiers for the indirect predicates, and the Stage-0 planar overlay runs in `RBig` (`coplanar_overlay.rs`, `stage0/*.rs`). Stage 3/4 geometry is floating with bands, which is inherent to the paper (d_p). Two gaps: (a) `Sign::from_f64` maps NaN to `Zero`, and the rational tier then calls `FBig::try_from(x).expect(...)` (`orient.rs:145-146`), so a NaN coordinate reaching a predicate panics; I found no finiteness validation of input coordinates in `kernel-v2/src/adapter.rs` or `boolean/to_yang.rs`. **Definitely a missing boundary check, effort S.** (b) The four release-active switches named by N100 item 8 (`CHERCHI_GRAZE_AWARE_RAY`, `CHERCHI_SCE_SHORTCIRCUIT`, `YANG_S1_SELF_CONTACT`, `_CHART`) still change results when set and two are absent from the probe registry; delete or register them.

**Tolerances.** The A14.3 rule (three canonical tolerances defined once) holds in `waffle-types/src/kernel/units.rs` and not in `yang-rs`: production code there contains **141 distinct `1e-N` literals**; `1e-9` appears 92 times as a literal and `TAU_EVAL` is referenced 37 times for the same band (e.g. `stage1_tessellate.rs:775,826,996`, `stage4_relocate.rs:631,759`). The 2026-10-22 review praised the centralized policy; at the kernel layer it is not centralized. **Definitely wrong against the project's own invariant, effort M** (mechanical, but each site needs a decision about which band it is).

**Panics in production paths.** `crates/yang-rs/CLAUDE.md` rule 6 says none. I count about 60 `expect`/`unwrap` calls outside test modules (`stage4_splice.rs` ×9, `stage4_correct/collapse.rs` ×9 including `collapse.rs:224`, `stage5_topology/construct.rs` ×7, `stage0/reloc.rs` ×6 plain `unwrap`s). In the browser a panic is a WASM trap with no unwind: the engine instance dies and the app has to detect it. Each site is probably a true invariant; "probably" is the problem. **Effort M; convert to typed `YangError` returns or document the invariant at the call site.**

**Degenerate and coincident cases.** Coplanar planes, coincident cylinders, 0D kisses, 1D tangent generators, sub-feature slivers below `MIN_FEATURE_SIZE` and minimizer-minted contacts all have named, loud outcomes (the C-series walls, P0026, C0107–C0109). The policy that a 0D kiss between solids is an ERROR rather than a non-manifold union is right for a 2-manifold kernel. What is still silent is listed honestly in N100 (items 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 open) and now has a census probe; item 3 (`kernel-v2/src/recover.rs:254-270` shipping a curved rim as a chord with no signal) is the one I would close first because it violates A15.5 outright.

**Topology consistency.** Strong: `validate_solid` with Euler–Poincaré, per-operand closed-manifold gate (I3a), T4 non-manifold mesh, T3b output curve crossings, and since 10-28 T8 on-surface vertices. T8's finding that corpus case R0099 had shipped a vertex 7.3e-3 off its surface and been graded correct is the most important single data point of the week (see §2).

**f32 / f64.** The kernel is f64 throughout and the only f32 is the render mesh (`waffle_types::RenderMesh.vertices: Vec<f32>`, cast at `kernel-v2/src/adapter.rs:183-205`). There is no WebGPU anywhere in the app; rendering is `three.WebGLRenderer` via Threlte (`viewport/Viewport.svelte:27`), so the question of WebGPU-versus-native float handling does not arise. The real f32 issue is the opposite direction: f32 presentation constraints have leaked into kernel tessellation policy — `render_subresolution_triangle`, `render_height_floor = 4·max|c|·f32::EPSILON` (`tessellate/mod.rs:736-769`), the f32 dedup grid `TAU_TESS_GRID_FACTOR`, and now a graded Bowyer–Watson collar in `developable.rs` justified by "17 non-manifold f32 render edges" on C0116. **Judgment call, confidence high:** emit render meshes relative to a per-body (or per-tab) origin computed in f64 and pass the origin to the renderer; large-coordinate f32 collapse disappears and the tessellator stops carrying presentation heuristics. Effort M.

### 1.6 Literature worth adopting (beyond what `REFERENCES.md` already lists)

- **Persistent naming has no entry in `REFERENCES.md`** although GeomRef naming, drawing anchors and assembly mates all depend on it. Adopt Kripac 1997 ("A mechanism for persistently naming topological entities"), Capoyleas–Chen–Hoffmann 1996 ("Generic naming in generative, constraint-based design") and Marcheix–Pierra 2002 (the survey) before touching naming again; the drawing anchor ambiguity in §3.3 is a textbook instance of the problem they solve.
- **Yang–Jia–Yan 2023 (topology-guaranteed SSI, ref #25)** as a second, independent loop detector: its characteristic-point count is an oracle for the Gauss-map admission test and for the "multiple small loops in one region" limitation the paper itself names (`:1097-1105`). Use it as a test oracle first, as the Stage-3 path for B-spline pairs when F5 lands.
- **Spainhour–Weiss 2026 (GWN over trimmed NURBS, ref #30)** as the classification oracle for the first non-analytic surface instance: it classifies without tessellation, so it can adjudicate the mesh-based verdict on B-spline faces where no exact volume exists.
- **Guo & Fu 2024 (indirect offset predicates, cited by Yang `:166-169`)** addresses filter failure on large coordinate terms, which is the N99/N24 family; worth reading before any further filter work in `cherchi-rs`.
- **Fudos–Hoffmann 1997 / Hoffmann–Lomonosov–Sitharam 2001 (already #47–#48)** should be *implemented*, not only cited, as a graph-decomposition pre-pass that gives the sketcher DOF and conflict diagnosis independent of the numeric solver (see §3.2).

## 2. Test coverage

### 2.1 What the assay corpus and its oracles really catch and miss

The corpus is 374 cases (C 118 constructed, F 94 user files, P 62 prospector-minted, R 100 generated). One verdict function, `categorize` (`crates/test-harness/src/assay/categorize.rs:157-516`), serves the corpus, the prospector and the CI smoke pins, and **it grades a render mesh, never the output B-Rep**. Per case it checks, in order: typed `NotSupported` text, engine errors, auto-union failures, a tessellation at tolerance `clamp(scale·0.01, 1e-9, 0.1)`, mesh checks (watertight with an exact-bit fast path, winding and positive signed volume, degenerate triangles, face-range coverage, per-body self-intersection), a minimum triangle count, volume magnitude in `[1e-8, 1e8]·scale³`, `expected_volume` at **1e-3 relative by default** (5e-2 on 42 curved metas; `categorize.rs:301-317`, verified), Euler characteristic with shells, bounding-box sanity, solid count or boss-volume composition, and the exact-membership lattice volume at 256 cells with a band of ≥ 0.5 %. Authored geometric expectations exist for 117 cases (C and P series only); **the F and R series carry none** and rely on the lattice when it covers the document.

What this catches well: gross set errors (dropped cavities, no-op cuts, complementary wedges), non-manifold or open render meshes, wrong genus where χ has been adjudicated, and silently empty bodies. What it cannot see (verified by reading `categorize.rs`, `oracle.rs`, `validate/faces.rs`):

### 3.2 Sketcher — the numerics are sound; the JS↔Rust seams are where it breaks

**State.** The solver (`crates/sketch-solver`) is a dense Levenberg–Marquardt with hand-written analytic Jacobians (finite-difference unit checks), proximal rows for drag stability, column-pivoted QR rank, and a full state report (dof, rank, residuals, conflicts, redundant, free, moved). It is deterministic. It has no graph decomposition, no sparsity, no Newton phase; splines and ellipses are not solver entities; tangency exists only line–circle/arc. The data model is sound and the earlier "`apply_sketch` drops arcs" defect is fixed (`waffle-types/src/sketch.rs:273-310`, pinned by `test-harness/tests/sketch_arc_rederive.rs`; only a stale comment at `wasm-bridge/src/tools/sketch.rs:866-870` survives). The MCP surface (`sketch_create`, `sketch_edit`, `sketch_solve_state`, `sketch_regions`) is strong: an agent can create every entity type, add every constraint type, read the full solver report and exact positions, and verify a sketch without a screenshot; the e2e notes record 87 closed-form checks.

**Definitely wrong (all verified by me in the code):**
1. **Right-click "Symmetric" breaks solving for the rest of the session.** `constraintLogic.js:206` emits `entity_c`; the Rust type wants `symmetry_line` (`waffle-types/src/sketch.rs:669-673`); `mapConstraintForBridge` (`store.svelte.js:3209-3245`) does not translate it; serde rejects the add, and because `triggerSolve` resends the whole constraint list, every later solve fails too. No GUI test covers symmetric-about-line. Effort S (one field name).
2. **Angle sign convention mismatch.** The UI measures angles unsigned (`acos`, `constraintLogic.js:110-128`, `dimensionHeuristic.js:72-82`); the solver residual is signed `atan2` with no ±π wrap (`constraint_mapping.rs:978-989`). For clockwise pairs, "dimension the current angle" demands the mirror angle and moves geometry; 180° is unreachable from one side. Both solver angle tests use CCW fixtures, and the rank oracle shares the signed convention, so nothing can disagree. Effort S–M.
3. **Metre-valued magic numbers eat small geometry.** The circle tool drops any radius ≤ 1 mm with no toast (`tools.js:915`, `radius > 0.001`); the slot tool resets below 1 mm (`tools.js:2345`); right-click "Distance" on point + line applies `value: 1.0`, i.e. one metre, instantly (`constraintLogic.js:153`), as do `hDist || 1.0` / `vDist || 1.0`. A 0.8 mm PCB hole cannot be drawn with the circle tool. Effort S.
4. **One absolute tolerance over mixed units.** `SOLVE_TOL = 1e-6` (`solver.rs:35`) thresholds residuals in metres (coincident, distance), square metres (parallel and perpendicular use the raw cross/dot product, `constraint_mapping.rs:893-906`; tangent uses `dist² − r²`) and radians (angle). At mm scale the area rows under-report: two 10 mm lines read "parallel, satisfied" at about 0.57° off, 3 mm lines at about 6°, and the conflict ranking compares areas with lengths. The large-scale half of this defect is pinned as known-open (`sketch_rank_oracle.rs:1247, :1327`); the small-scale half is not. Effort M: normalize every residual to length units and make `SOLVE_TOL`/`MOVED_EPS` relative to sketch extent.
5. **Over-constraint colouring is a count heuristic.** Any point or line referenced by more than two constraints is painted over-constrained (`store.svelte.js:4414-4440`, verified) regardless of the solver's verdict; a fully constrained corner (coincident plus two distances) shows red.

**Judgment calls:** the dense Jacobian is rebuilt and cloned per LM evaluation, a ColPivQR rank and a `SymmetricEigen(JᵀJ)` run per solve, the redundancy walk is super-cubic (the code's own comment measures 669 ms for 100 points, native release), and `dragSketchPoint` calls `triggerSolve` on every pointer move without coalescing — while the UI reads only `dof` and `conflicts` from the report, so the expensive parts are computed for nobody. `sketch/profiles.js` and `finishProfiles.js` duplicate the Rust region finder on the live path (the active sketch uses JS regions, inactive sketches and the MCP door use the engine's), so profile indices depend on two different tie-breaks. `conflicts` is "every constraint with |r| > tol", not a minimal conflicting set, so cause and victim are indistinguishable for an agent. `MovePoint` over MCP pins at full weight while a user drag is weighted 1/20 — the same gesture with a different answer. The independent sketch oracles (`sketch_corpus`, `sketch_rank_oracle`, `agent_sketch_edit_survival`, `sketch_arc_rederive`) do not run in CI.

**The three changes that would do the most:** (1) dimensionless verdicts — length-normalized residuals and extent-relative tolerances; (2) one constraint contract — generate the JS builders from the Rust schema already exported in `engineSchemas.generated.js`, delete `mapConstraintForBridge`, measure dimension defaults in Rust; (3) put the report to work and make it cheap — highlight `free` points and `redundant` constraints, return a minimal conflicting set from one factorization, drop the super-cubic walk, coalesce drag solves. Tests to add first: the Symmetric regression, a clockwise-pair angle test, a 0.5 mm circle at mm units, a scale sweep for parallel/perpendicular/tangent conflict detection, JS-versus-Rust profile parity over `app/tests/cases/sketch/`, and the sketch oracles in `rust-test.yml`.

## 4. Architecture and development approach

### 4.1 Boundaries, the parametric model, and the file format

**Kernel/app boundary — clean at compile time, accreted at the contract.** No consumer crate imports a `kernel-v2` symbol except one DXF shortcut in the bridge (`wasm-bridge/src/dispatch.rs:1665-1688, :2675` call `kernel_v2::dxf_export` directly although `KernelProjection::export_dxf` exists — JUDGMENT-CALL, S); `KernelV2Adapter` is the only entry. The contract itself is 52 methods over four traits (`Kernel` 24, `KernelIntrospect` 18, `KernelMeasure` 5, `KernelProjection` 5), **27 of them defaulted to `NotSupported`** — one-off doors added per feature rather than a designed surface. Sketch-domain types leak into it (`make_faces_from_profiles(&HashMap<u32,(f64,f64)>)` at `traits.rs:216`, `make_face_from_region(&Region)` at `:231`, `sweep(&Chain3d)` at `:275`). The only required methods without a default are fillet, chamfer and shell — the three deferred operations — stubbed in `adapter.rs:623-653` with 266 lines of `modeling-ops/src/{fillet,chamfer,shell}.rs` behind them; make them default-`NotSupported` and delete the stubs (S). Render tessellation returns f32 through the kernel trait and the f32 dedup grid lives in kernel `units.rs:70-75`; acceptable for a browser CAD since measurement and export never read the mesh, but see §1.4 for the direction the coupling runs.

**Parametric history and regeneration.**
- **Incremental rebuild leaves `UnionAll { All }` and `PatternSeeds::All` stale.** DEFINITELY-WRONG, high, S, verified. A non-rerun feature executes only if `scan_references` finds a rerun UUID or `depends_on_tree_position` says so (`feature-engine/src/rebuild.rs:231-244`); that predicate returns `false` for every operation except Extrude/Revolve/Pipe/Sweep (`rebuild.rs:490-513`), and `UnionTargets::All` / `PatternSeeds::All` serialize no UUID (`types.rs:1831`, `:1463`). Edit a seed's sketch: the seed re-executes, the union keeps its carried result (`:252-262`) and re-marks the fresh bodies consumed (`:283-305`), so a stale lump renders. No test covers it (`test-harness/tests/incremental_rebuild_kv2.rs` compares triangle counts only).
- **Failure semantics are soft.** JUDGMENT-CALL, high, S–M. There is no `FeatureStatus`: a failed feature records an error, gets no result, and the rebuild continues (`rebuild.rs:422-434`); positional consumers (legacy most-recent, share-a-face, `All`) then bind the previous live body silently. A cut whose tool misses the target returns the target with `Diagnostics::default()` (`modeling-ops/src/boolean.rs:141-145`, verified) — a silent no-op; the blind-cut auto-reverse falls to `None => true` when nothing is measurable (`rebuild.rs:913-921`); and auto-union failure is detected downstream by the substring `"Auto-union failed"` (`rebuild.rs:293-298`, verified). These are the engine-side roots of the agent-facing silences in §3.5.
- **GeomRef persistent naming is less than its document claims.** JUDGMENT-CALL, high, M (reviewer's reading, load-bearing lines re-read). `Selector` is Role / Signature / Query / Position / Pid (`geom_ref.rs:124-166`) with `BestEffort` the default policy; there is no cross-variant order, so the documented "role then signature" fallback does not exist. `resolve_by_pid` correctly refuses a split face under both policies, but `resolve_with_fallback` binds the *first created entity of the kind* under `BestEffort` (`resolve.rs:584-597`, `ResolvedVia::KindFallback`), signature matching binds below its own 50 % floor under `BestEffort` (`:1179-1190`), `Provenance.modified` is never populated (`modeling-ops/src/boolean.rs:137`), and lineage is one level deep. `docs/PERSISTENT-NAMING.md` (last touched 2026-02-08) describes truck, three strategies in order and a signature fallback — wrong in substance; rewrite it from `resolve.rs`, and read the naming literature first (§1.6).
- **Body custody is positional.** JUDGMENT-CALL, med, L. Body ids are `{feature}/{Main|Body:N}` with N the kernel's lump order; a multi-target cut re-indexes; unnamed siblings are carried as the consumer's new `Body{index}` with a warning; reorder is `Changed::All`. There is no lump provenance, so a legacy cut's target can change under reorder.
- **Assemblies are fine for what they are:** rigid instances, connector frames from live geometry, Fastened by composition, other mates by damped Gauss–Newton over all mates at once (`assembly_solver.rs:124-329`), no DOF or redundancy analysis.

**File format (`.waffle`, format 15).** JSON recipe is the right container today and `docs/FILE_FORMAT.md` is the best single document in the repo, but:
- **Saves are not byte-deterministic.** DEFINITELY-WRONG, high, S, verified. `body_names`, `provenance`, `body_materials` are `HashMap` (`feature-engine/src/types.rs:14, 44, 143`); `solved_positions` serializes through a `HashMap<String,_>`; the writer is `serde_json::to_string_pretty` from the structs (`file-format/src/save.rs:215-227`), and `SaveVerifier`'s own comment says "the same tab serializes differently run to run" (`save.rs:269-275`). Every sketch's key order churns in git diffs. Fix: `BTreeMap` plus a sorted key serializer, format-neutral.
- **Derived data dominates the file.** DEFINITELY-WRONG against the spec's own §1 intent, high, M. The root `Bike frame.waffle.json` is 23.7 MB with 549,695 floats, zero embeds or meshes, 42 sketches and 18 regions; the floats are the regions' tessellated `outer`/`holes` polylines double-encoded beside `outer_edges` (§10.3 / §14.9 of the spec already names it, ~374 KB per region). Also persisted and derived: `solved_positions`, `solved_profiles`, `preview_mesh`, drawing view caches, assembly placements. Drop `outer` when `outer_edges` is present behind one reader-floor bump, or group derived blocks under one key a loader may discard.
- **The spec contradicts the code.** DEFINITELY-WRONG, high, S, verified. `docs/FILE_FORMAT.md:3` says format 9; `save.rs:163` writes 15 (`:136-137` say 6 and 14); the schema golden is titled "v5" at format 15 and the CI step calls it "v4". The schema itself is current and enforced in CI (`rust-test.yml:127`); only its name lies. There is no runtime schema validation on load; serde is the validator, which is fine.
- **Room for photos and KiCad.** JUDGMENT-CALL, med. The `sources` table (git / url / local / `Embedded` locators, deflate-base64 payloads, git-blob-sha1 dedup, `sources.rs:396-422`) can hold a photo today, but base64 inside pretty-printed JSON plus the save-time re-parse (`save.rs:234-238`) is the wrong home above a few megabytes. Keep JSON as the recipe; when the first binary payload lands, move `embed` payloads to a sibling blob store (a zip of JSON plus blobs as 3MF does, or files beside the document in git). The locator model already abstracts location, so this is M, not L; do not switch containers before a binary payload exists. KiCad: `specs/kicad_board_link.md` C1–C5 all landed — outline with arcs, stackup thickness, footprints, pads and drills, mounting-hole rule, board extrude with cutouts, placeholder boxes, component STEP models matched by name, re-sync by `external_key`; not implemented: courtyards (no `CrtYd` reader), heights only via the board STEP, and none of it is exposed as data over MCP (§3.5).
- **STEP is one parser with two tiers, not two paths:** truck parses (three git dependencies pinned to one master revision — a supply risk, L to replace), `parse_step_tiered` yields exact `AnalyticShellData` for `kernel_v2::ingest` or mesh `ImportedBodyData`; export is native `kernel_v2::step_export`. Asymmetric, not duplicated.

### 4.3 Is the agent-driven process converging on a robust kernel?

On the kernel core, yes — and that is not a given for a codebase of 632 k lines of Rust written mostly by agents. The evidence for convergence is specific: the 2026-10-14 cleanup replaced three refinement ladders with the paper's one loop and deleted 32 kill switches; `stage4_correct.rs` went from 15,142 lines (2026-10-22 review) to 6,863 by extraction into `collapse.rs`, `corner_transit.rs`, `reversal.rs` and `domain_postcondition.rs`; the compliance matrix was re-graded *against* the project's interest; red-first pins are the norm in commit messages; independent reviews (B1–S10 on F4) are run and acted on; misreadings (N96, N97, N89 item 3) are found by re-reading the paper; the user's sign-offs are recorded in prose with the exact words; and the rule "a substitute that would convert a case is not built" is being obeyed (F0064 has waited since 10-25 as a milestone customer).

The evidence against is also specific, and it all comes from one week of parallel agents:

- **Verification did not keep up with velocity.** 59 commits landed on 10-27. The CI Rust job was **cancelled at its 120-minute limit on five consecutive pushes (16:08–21:14)** while F4, F6 and the literature-check merges continued; the push hook is advisory (`scripts/hooks/pre_push_ci_check.sh` prints the last five runs as context, nothing blocks). The N99 commit's own message reported two corpus TIMEOUTs and attributed them to SMT contention; the actual cause was the 2× Cherchi classify regression that commit introduced, found a day later by the next session. The F4 merge landed with its integration suites not run (`6714be2f` says so) and carried, for part of a day, a path that adopted a silent-wrong body. None of these became a WRONG in the corpus, which is partly the point: the corpus could not have seen them.
- **New mechanisms arrive with new tunables.** The cleanup retired env knobs and arms; F4 added ~130 named constants, 15 `#[allow(clippy::too_many_arguments)]`, 27 `#[allow(dead_code)]` in yang-rs (unwired I3a primitives), 24 reads of the test harness's `ASSAY_CASE` inside kernel crates, and 141 distinct `1e-N` literals remain in yang-rs production code. The fragility has moved, not gone.
- **Order of work is being chosen for parallelism, not dependency.** F2 is the critical path for F1 (I3b), F3 (Case-IV, locality), F5 (d_ε) and the retirement of every substitute; the roadmap admits this. Yet the week's effort went to F4, F5 steps 1–2, F6, the literature check and loft, each of which added code that F2 must later re-home (the small-loop splice is a fourth §4.4.1 site; `LocalSurfaceChart` and `GaussMap` are a per-type prototype of F5's interface). Parallel agents are being given parallel milestones because they are available, and the serial milestone is starving.
- **Housekeeping debt is now operational risk.** `target/` is 254 GB, `/home/claude/wt` 80 GB, 41 stale `.claude/worktrees/agent-*` checkouts, the disk at 92 %; a full disk mid-corpus-run produces spurious TIMEOUTs that look like regressions. Root-level clutter flagged on 10-22 is still there; `README.md` and `STATUS.md` still describe a deleted kernel; `REVIEW.md` was a six-month-old auto-generated note. Markdown is 210 k lines, more than the app code, and there are 429 specs — a newcomer or a fresh agent cannot find the plan of record without the memory file this session happens to have.

**Verdict (judgment call, confidence high):** the kernel work is converging because the governance (paper-as-spec, ledger, matrix, loud ceilings, sign-offs) is doing its job; the process around it is accumulating risk because the gates are advisory and the agents are many. The fix is mechanical, not cultural: a merge gate that requires green CI on the base and `test.sh rewrite` on the branch, one serial owner for F2, and a cap on parallel kernel milestones until F2 lands.
